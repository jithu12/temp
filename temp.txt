“See, in this kind of scenario, the first thing that happens is that the Accounts Team, from their application side, will change the account status to **Deactivated**.

Once they change the status to **Deactivated** on their side, the next thing that happens is part of the lifecycle flow.

They will publish a lifecycle event to the Event Bus called **Resource Disabled (LifecycleEvent.ResourceDisabled)**.
That means a `Resource Disabled` event will be pushed to the Event Bus.


What we need to do is listen to the Event Bus and read that event. Once we receive the `Disabled` event, we should update the account status to **Deactivated** on our side as well.

also during the grace period owner can also change the status to Active (LifecycleEvent.ResourceActive)

Okay? Is this clear so far, bro?

The next step is: after the Accounts Team’s grace period is over, we do not need to do anything manually. Once the grace period ends, the Accounts Team themselves will move the account to the **Deleting (LifecycleEvent.ResourceDeleting)** state.

So initially the account will be in the **Deactivated** state, and later the Accounts Team will update it to the **Deleting** state.

At that point, we will receive another event from the Event Bus called **Resource Deleting (LifecycleEvent.ResourceDeleting)**.

Whenever we receive the `Resource Deleting` event, we should delete the account on our side as well.

Okay? Understood?

We are not maintaining any grace period logic on our side. That responsibility belongs completely to the Accounts Team. After the grace period, they move the account to the `Deleting` state, and then the Event Bus sends us the `Resource Deleting` event. Once we receive it, we delete the resource on our side.

One more thing — sometimes the Event Bus crawler or processor may miss certain events.

To handle that, we should have a daily scheduled job or periodic sync process.

That scheduled run should verify and reconcile all accounts after a certain interval and mark/update anything that was missed.

That’s all we need to handle on our side.”
